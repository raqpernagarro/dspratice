---
name: repurpose
description: Turns one of the author's saved pieces in brands/me/content/ (a LinkedIn post or article, a Substack essay, an Instagram post or an X thread) into children for the other networks, and a LinkedIn post into a Viva Engage post for Nagarro colleagues (-viva, always nagarro mode). It gives each target one insight from the source in the format that suits it, writes it from the source's facts alone, and saves it with parent set to the source, on the source's day at the surface's usual time, clean under scripts/check.py. An existing child is never overwritten. One labelled needs-caption gets its caption in place with scripts/set_body.py, and any other is reported as exists and skipped. Use when the author asks to "repurpose" a piece, for "the Instagram version" or "the Viva Engage version", to "turn this into a thread", for "the other networks" or to "write the captions for the DS posts". Not for a new idea (use ideation), an article or essay (use write-longform) or an idea's first drafts (use write-social-post).
---

# repurpose

Takes one saved piece, the source, and writes its children for the other networks, as `marketeer/references/repurposing-matrix.md` sets out: each child carries one insight from the source, in the format that suits it, written for its network and never trimmed from the source. A new child is saved with `scripts/new_post.py`, an Instagram child still waiting for its caption is completed with `scripts/set_body.py`, and every child passes `scripts/check.py` before the author sees it. Paths are relative to the repository root.

## Inputs

In any order and wording (`ds-07-two-versions, instagram and x` is enough):

- A source id: the file name of `brands/me/content/<id>.md` without `.md`, a `type: post` the matrix lists as a source: a LinkedIn post (any format but `article`), a LinkedIn `article`, a Substack `essay`, an Instagram post or an X `thread`. An idea (`type: idea`) is not a source: offer `write-social-post`.
- A child id in place of a source: when the author names a child (`ds-07-two-versions-ig`), its `parent` is the source and its surface the only target.
- "The captions for the DS posts", or every piece still waiting for a caption: list them with `grep -l "needs-caption" brands/me/content/*.md` and take each one as a child id, one source after another. Step 1 runs once when they share pillar, mode and campaign.
- Targets: `linkedin`, `instagram`, `x`, `substack` or `viva`, or "the other networks". With none, every derivative the matrix's "Source to derivatives" table lists for the source's kind, `viva` only when the source is a LinkedIn post dated on a Tuesday or a Thursday. A target on the source's own surface is refused, except the promo post of a LinkedIn article. `viva` takes only a LinkedIn post (any format but `article`) as its source: from anything else it is refused, with the reason in one line.
- A format for a target ("as a thread"): it wins over step 4's match when the surface file allows it; say so in one line when the insight it carries suits another format in the matrix.
- `--force`: only when the author asks for it in the session, to write an existing child again (step 5).

## Steps

1. Run `marketeer-context` (`/marketeer:marketeer-context`) with the source's `pillar`, `mode` and `campaign` from its frontmatter, and the source id as the task sentence. Name the surface only when there is a single target; with several, name none, so `kit.md` is read when Instagram is among them. When `viva` is a target beside others, run it once more for that child with surface `viva` and mode `nagarro`: its block carries the internal rule in place of the overlay's promotion lines. When `viva` is the only target, the run above already names it as the surface, and the context resolves the mode to `nagarro`, so do not run it a second time. Work from its block: the four Voice rows and the two swipe files on the `Swipe:` line, Hard rules, Mode, Campaign (its tags and sources), Facts available and Gaps.
2. Read, in full:
   - the source: the body, and the frontmatter `title`, `surface`, `format`, `mode`, `campaign`, `pillar`, `scheduled_date`, `first_comment`, `sources`, `tags_people` and `assets`;
   - `marketeer/references/repurposing-matrix.md`, `marketeer/references/ai-tells.md` and `marketeer/references/hooks.md`;
   - `marketeer/references/surfaces/<surface>.md` for each target, down to its `## Checklist`, which is what `check.py` runs (`surfaces/viva-engage.md` for `viva`); `marketeer/references/longform.md` when the source or a child is long form or points to it (an article, an essay, a promo post, a note);
   - `brands/me/kit.md` for an Instagram target; `brands/me/nagarro-overlay.md` in `nagarro` mode; `brands/me/campaigns/<slug>.md` when the source has a campaign, for its `required_hashtags_<surface>` keys and its dates; the two swipe files from the block. When the source is itself a swipe post (`ds-07` is the text of `ds-07-two-versions`), it is the source, not the bar.

   Then work out each target's child id from the matrix's Ids section: the source id minus its own ending (`-li`, `-ig` or `-x` on that surface, `-article`, `-essay`), plus the child's suffix (`-ig`, `-x`, `-li`, `-note`, `-promo`, `-viva`). Look for what already exists with `ls brands/me/content/<stem>-*.md` and `grep -l "^parent: <source-id>$" brands/me/content/*.md`. Only `<stem>` plus a target's suffix counts as that target's child: anything else the `ls` lists (a `-reel`, a `-promo` when the target is X, another piece whose id starts with the stem) is not this run's business. For each existing child, note `labels_extra`, `format`, `assets`, `visual_brief` and `trypost.post_id`. When it has an image in `assets` (a path inside `brands/me/`), open the image and write down its on-image copy word for word: that card is fixed, and the caption is written around it.
3. Extract the insights to a scratch file (step 5 says where) and rank them:
   - 3 to 7 insights, one sentence each. Under each: the source's lines it rests on, its type from the matrix's "Insight types to formats" table, and the source behind any fact in it (the source's `sources`, or the article its first comment names).
   - The facts are the source's: its body, its first comment and its `sources`. The article a source cites is where those facts come from, not a store of new ones: a child says about it only what the source says, and you never open a URL to find more. A fact keeps its figures, subject, scope and verbs ("rarely says so" never becomes "never says"), and a number keeps its source in its sentence or in `sources`.
   - The anchor first: the insight the source is built on, usually its hook or its thesis. Rank the rest by how well each stands alone for a reader who never saw the source.
   - What an existing child's card already says is that child's insight. The card decides, not the ranking. Its illustrative details (a price, a route, a field name in a mock-up) are the designer's example, not facts: a caption may point at them ("the question marks on the right") but states no figure from the card.
4. Match each target to one insight and one format, the anchor first, then draft it per its surface file:
   - The match: the insight's type gives the format through the matrix's table (a counterintuitive claim makes an X `text` post or an Instagram `image`; step by step makes an X `thread` or a carousel; a framework makes a carousel). An existing child keeps its format and the insight its card carries. Each other target takes the anchor when no card already holds it, and otherwise the highest-ranked insight left, in the format its type gives on that surface: on X, a claim that stands alone in 280 characters is a `text` post, and one that needs steps is a `thread`. One child, one insight: support it with the source's mechanism, but never walk through the source's paragraphs in order or try to carry every insight.
   - Write it fresh for the network. A child shares the source's facts and closing intent, not its sentences: any sentence found word for word in the source, other than a quoted fact, is rewritten; so is a run of paraphrased sentences in the source's order. Restating the source's paragraphs in its order, each reworded, is still a trimmed copy: come at the insight from another side than the source does (the reader's own offer, what the agent tells the customer, the detail on the card), with a hook from `hooks.md`'s list for that network, and paraphrase at most the one fact the insight rests on. The children differ from each other the same way: no sentence, no sentence frame ("may not be the one you ...", "the agent acting for your customer") and no closing question in two of them, reworded or not; each child has its own closing.
   - Instagram (`surfaces/instagram.md`): the text on the card is the first hook. The caption's first 125 characters carry a second one that picks up the card's words and promises an angle the card does not give, never a repeat. Warmer and shorter than LinkedIn, the same voice: one short sentence per line, a blank line between ideas, "→" for lists. Vary the blocks: a run of equal two-line blocks is the uniform rhythm `ai-tells.md` warns about, so mix a one-line block and a longer sentence among the pairs. It stands alone: a reader who sees only Instagram gets the point. No link and never "link in the comments": "Link in bio" at most (or "linked in my bio"), and only for a link the source itself points to, when the source's closing intent is to send the reader there; `first_comment` stays empty. Up to 5 hashtags on the last line, the campaign's `required_hashtags_instagram` included; in the Design Systems campaign `#FluidicIntelligence` belongs to LinkedIn only. People as plain names, never @handles unless the author supplies them. A new `image` or `carousel` child also gets `visual_brief` and `image_prompt` as `write-social-post` step 4 sets them out; a carousel brief names the kit family of every slide: a cover, 3 to 5 interiors from family A (one B, D or E among them when the argument is visual), then the closing card 38, 8 slides at most.
   - X (`surfaces/x.md`): `text` is one post of 280 characters or fewer, and aim under 240 so the author can add a word; `thread` is 4 to 8 posts of 280 or fewer, separated by a line holding only `---` with a blank line on each side. The first post stands alone, hook and point, worth reading if nobody opens the rest; no "1/", no "Thread", no numbering on any post. One idea per post. The single post, or the last one, ends with a concrete question the reader answers from their own work or one action for this week, then 0 or 1 hashtag. The link goes in `first_comment`, the reply: the source's first comment reworded for X, inside a sentence that says what it is, with the URL kept exactly.
   - LinkedIn, from an X thread, an essay or an article (`surfaces/linkedin.md` and the matrix's promo rules): the hook in lines 1 and 2, the campaign's `required_hashtags_linkedin`, and `@Name` only where the overlay asks for a tag in `nagarro` mode. A promo post's `first_comment` stays empty until the author gives the live article URL: say so, and never write a placeholder.
   - Substack note (`surfaces/substack.md`): 2 to 4 plain sentences, no link, no hashtag.
   - Viva Engage (`surfaces/viva-engage.md`), from a LinkedIn post: the field note behind the public post, written as the author to people who share the company. Take the anchor insight and come at it from inside: what we learned on the work, a tool or checklist colleagues can use this week (the whole of it, never a teaser), the story behind a decision, or a question for Nagarro's design community. Line 1 is a title on its own line, a claim or a question for colleagues; then short paragraphs, "→" for lists; about 1,200 characters, within 900 to 1,500. Client-safe as everywhere: the lesson, never the client. No pitch: no offering sold, no "Nagarro offers", no call to book; an offering may be named as a fact or its internal page linked as a resource. No hashtags, the campaign's included; no `@Name` and no `--tags-people`; links inline, in the sentence that says what they are; `first_comment` empty. Close with a question colleagues answer from their own projects, or an ask for their examples. The format is `text`, or `image` or `video` when the source has a card or a video the author can attach, named in `visual_brief`.
   - The born-clean pass, before saving, as `write-social-post` step 6 sets it out. British English. No em or en dash in the body, title, first comment, brief or prompt. No banned word in any form, "screens" included (`ds-07`'s "three screens deep" is paraphrased with its meaning kept). No banned pattern, no stacked fragments, at most one question answered in the next line. No absolute claim ("guaranteed", "guarantee" and "guarantees" outside "guarantee nothing", "guarantees nothing" and "no guarantee", "100%", "#1", matched as whole words). "Comment" never within 60 characters of a word in capitals or a quoted word. Count each body with `python3 -c "import sys; print(len(open(sys.argv[1], encoding='utf-8').read().strip()))" <scratch file>` (each thread post on its own), and print a caption's cut with `python3 -c "import sys; print(open(sys.argv[1], encoding='utf-8').read()[:125])" <scratch file>`.
5. Save each child. Write each body, and each first comment, visual brief and image prompt, with the Write tool to its own scratch file in the session's scratchpad directory (a temporary directory when there is none). Never pass a body inline or through an unquoted heredoc; pass the other fields as `"$(cat <file>)"`.
   - An existing child whose `labels_extra` holds `needs-caption`: `python3 scripts/set_body.py <child-id> --body-file <scratch file> --remove-label needs-caption`. It prints `updated <child-id>` and changes only the body, that label and `updated`, and the gate: an `in-review` child goes back to `draft`, scored or not (the import left its Instagram children `in-review` with no score), and loses any score, and a scored `draft` loses its score, while an `approved` or `scheduled` child keeps its status and its score, given to the old caption. The TryPost link, format, assets, dates, review flags and every other field stay. It sets no first comment, brief or prompt; when the child needs one of those, give the author the value instead.
   - Any other existing child: print `exists <child-id>` and skip it, because the author may have edited it. Only with `--force` from the author is it written again. `new_post.py --force` rebuilds the whole object from the flags you pass and keeps only `trypost`, `status`, `posted_at`, `created`, `review` and `review_flags`: `assets`, `labels_extra`, `visual_brief`, `image_prompt` and every other field are lost unless passed again, and `new_post.py` has no flag for assets or labels. The gate runs as on a new body: an `in-review` child goes back to `draft` and loses any score, and an `approved` or `scheduled` one keeps its status and its score. So a child that carries `assets` or `labels_extra`, and any `approved` or `scheduled` child, is rewritten with `set_body.py <child-id> --body-file <scratch file>`, which touches only the body and what the gate changes; only a child with none of those is saved with the new-child command below plus `--force`, every field passed again as the source gives it.
   - A new child: `python3 scripts/new_post.py <child-id> --title "<title>" --surface <surface> --format <format> --mode <mode> --parent <source-id> [--pillar <n>] [--campaign <slug>] [--date <YYYY-MM-DD>] [--source <url>]... [--tags-people "<name>"]... [--first-comment "$(cat <file>)"] [--visual-brief "$(cat <file>)"] [--image-prompt "$(cat <file>)"] --body-file <scratch file>`. The id is the first argument, not a flag.
     - `--title`: the source's title as it stands, or "Promo: <article title>" and "Note: <essay title>" (the matrix). It holds no double quote, `$` or backtick.
     - `--mode`, `--pillar` and `--campaign`: the source's values; leave `--pillar` and `--campaign` out when the source has none. A `viva` child takes `--mode nagarro` whatever the source's mode, and leaves `--campaign` out when the source's campaign has `neutral: true` (`repurposing-matrix.md`, What carries over).
     - `--source`: once for each URL in the source's `sources`, all of them, in order. `--tags-people`: once for each name in the source's `tags_people`, as a plain name; never on a `viva` child.
     - `--date`: the first ten characters of the source's `scheduled_date`, which is stored in Lisbon time; `new_post.py` sets the surface's time on that day (LinkedIn 08:30, Instagram 12:30, X 09:30, Substack 09:00, Viva Engage 10:00). An undated source gives an undated child. A promo post takes its later day from the campaign (three days after the article in `flo-2026-talks`); with no rule there, ask the author.
     - `created <child-id>` is success. `exists <child-id> (use --force)` means a child is there that step 2 missed: it falls under the two cases above. `<child-id>: <problem>` means nothing was written: fix what it names and run it again.
6. Run `python3 scripts/check.py <child-id> ...` with every child this run wrote. For each `<child-id>: <problem>` line, fix the scratch file and save again: the same `set_body.py` command for a child this run captioned, and for one it created, `new_post.py` with every flag of step 5 again plus `--force`. Repeat until each prints `ok <child-id>`. Then read each child once more for what `check.py` cannot see: "link in the comments" in a caption, a sentence copied from the source, a fact said more strongly than the source says it, an X first post that needs the rest to make sense.
7. Show the result:
   - the insights in rank order, the anchor marked, and which child carries which;
   - for each child: the id and path, `created`, `updated` or `exists`, the surface and format, `scheduled_date`, the body as saved with its character count (per post for a thread), and the first comment; for a captioned child, its `trypost.post_id`, unchanged; then the `ok` lines;
   - what the author has to do by hand: a caption that says "Link in bio" needs the link in her bio on the day; a captioned child that was `in-review` is a `draft` again, and one that was `approved` or `scheduled` keeps that status and its score, so say its new caption has not been scored or reviewed yet and, for an approved one, that the author's approval was given to the old caption and she re-reads it on the board;
   - the leverage order: which child the author posts first if she posts only one, with the reason in one line. Rank by these, in order, each breaking the tie the one before leaves: a child on one of the pillar's networks in `brand-profile.md`; one that carries the link the campaign's goal needs (an X reply or a LinkedIn first comment, never "Link in bio"); one whose visual already exists; the earlier time on the day;
   - one next step: "Score them with `/marketeer:score <child ids>`?"

## Outputs

- Each new child, `brands/me/content/<stem>-<suffix>.md`: `type: post`, `status: draft`, `parent: <source-id>`, the source's title, campaign, pillar, mode, `sources` and `tags_people` (a `viva` child: `mode: nagarro`, no neutral campaign, no `tags_people`), the surface's usual time on the source's day, and `first_comment` where the surface takes one (the X reply with the link; empty on Instagram).
- Each `needs-caption` child: the new caption, the label gone and `updated` moved; its TryPost link, assets and date as they were, and its status too, except an `in-review` child, which the gate sends back to `draft`.
- `ok <id>` from `scripts/check.py` for every child written.
- Nothing else changes: the source, the children reported as `exists` and the workspace files stay as they are, and the scratch files live outside the repository.

## Rules

- The voice stays, the register shifts: `voice.md`'s card on every network, warmer and shorter on Instagram, one point at a time on X.
- Nothing invented. A child carries only facts the source carries, each with its source, said no more strongly than the source says them: no anecdote, number, result, quote, client, colleague or admission the source does not hold.
- One child, one insight, written for its network: never a trimmed copy of the source and never its sentences.
- Never overwrite an existing child: a `needs-caption` one gets its caption through `set_body.py`, any other is `exists` unless the author asks for `--force`.
- An Instagram caption never says "link in the comments", and its `first_comment` stays empty. An X first post stands alone, and its link sits in the reply.
- The mode and the campaign carry over with their rules. In `nagarro` mode the overlay applies: `@Name` tags and `#FluidicIntelligence` on LinkedIn only, plain names elsewhere. In `personal` mode nothing is promoted. In a neutral campaign there is no Nagarro, FLO, talk, keynote, slides or "the room".
- A `viva` child is the exception: always `nagarro`, never a neutral campaign, and the internal rule instead of the overlay's promotion rules (`compliance.md`, Internal pieces). It is written to colleagues and sells nothing.
- British English in every word, and no em or en dashes anywhere, the visual brief and image prompt included.
- A new child starts as `draft`; a captioned child keeps its status, except an `in-review` one, which goes back to `draft`. The author publishes by hand; the system never posts.

## Boundaries

- No new ideas: a topic with no saved piece goes to `ideation`, then `write-social-post`.
- No long form: articles and essays come from `write-longform`. This skill writes only the short pieces around them (a promo post, a note, an X thread, an Instagram carousel brief).
- No scoring, no review and no approval: `score` rates the children, `brand-review` flags them, the author approves.
- No edits to the source, or to a child the author may have changed.
- No sync: it never calls the TryPost API or `scripts/trypost_sync.py`; the next push carries the children.
- Writes only through `scripts/new_post.py` and `scripts/set_body.py`, and only the children of the source it was given.
