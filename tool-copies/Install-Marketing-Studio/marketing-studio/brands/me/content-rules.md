# Content rules: [Your name]

Mechanical rules every draft must obey. `scripts/check.py` checks them deterministically (the rules themselves live in `evals/lib/checks.py`, so changing a rule here means changing it there too). Voice lives in `voice.md`.

## Language

- British English (en-GB): colour, optimise, licence, organisation.
- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere, including ranges. Write "0 to 3", never a dash between the numbers. Use commas, colons, full stops or parentheses instead.
- Short lines, one idea each. "→" for lists, never dash bullets. Numbered lists only for real sequences.

## Banned words and patterns

Hype words: game-changer, unlock, elevate, delve, leverage, landscape, crucial, pivotal, testament, showcase, quietly, vibrant, robust, seamless, thrilled, humbled, excited to announce.

Patterns: "it's not X, it's Y"; groups of three used for rhythm; a rhetorical question followed by its own answer more than once per piece; "Here's the thing"; "Let's dive in"; punchy one-line fragments stacked in a row; generic positive endings; "Thoughts?" or "Agree?" as a closer; vague claims such as "many companies", "studies show", "experts agree".

Giveaways gated behind a comment ("Comment GUIDE and I'll send it") are banned outright.

## Hashtags

- LinkedIn 0 to 3, plus any hashtag a campaign requires.
- Instagram up to 5.
- X 0 to 1.
- Hashtags go at the end of the text, never inside a sentence.

## Links

- LinkedIn: never in the body. The link goes in the first comment, and the body may say "link in the comments".
- X: never in the body. The link goes in a reply.
- Instagram: no links in captions. "Link in bio" at most.
- Substack and LinkedIn articles: links in the body are fine; sources go in footnotes or a Sources section.

## Emojis

Only functional ones, and few: a flag for a country, a pin for a location, an arrow for a list. No decoration, no emoji as a bullet, none in the first line.

## Numbers and sources

Every number, client name, award or quote carries its source in the sentence, in a footnote, or in the piece's `sources` field. Numbers without a source are removed, not softened. Where a figure is illustrative, the text says so.

## Confidentiality

- No client names, no project details, no personal data about anyone. Replace them with a neutral description ("a client", "a developer on my team") and keep the insight.
- A proof point from `brand-profile.md`, stated as written with its source, may be used.
- Facts come only from the author or a cited source. No invented anecdotes, numbers, results, quotes or credentials. When a detail is missing, ask the author.
- The system never posts. The author publishes by hand.

## Per-network notes

| Network | Rules that shape the draft |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Up to 3,000 characters; about 210 show before "see more", so the first two lines carry the hook. Aim for 900 to 1,500 characters for text posts. PDF carousels have 6 to 12 slides at 1080×1350. |
| Instagram | Caption up to 2,200 characters, about 125 visible. Every post needs an image, a carousel (4:5 or 3:4) or a Reel (9:16). |
| X | 280 characters per post; threads of 4 to 8 posts. |
| LinkedIn article | 1,000 to 1,400 words, practical and scannable, a cover at 1920×1080. |
| Substack essay | 1,800 to 2,500 words, footnotes for sources. |
| Substack note | A few lines: a promo for the essay or a standalone thought. |
