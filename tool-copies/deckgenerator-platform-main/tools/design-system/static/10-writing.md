# Writing for slides

## Voice

Plain, specific and confident: a senior consultant talking to a client across a table. Warm without being casual; expert without jargon. Nagarro calls itself a caring brand, and the writing should sound like people who care about the client's problem, not about sounding impressive.

## Titles

- A title makes a claim the slide proves: "Handlers spend a third of the day re-keying data", not "Current state".
- One line, two at most. The caption above it carries the topic ("Discovery", "Approach", "Plan").
- Sentence case. No full stop at the end of a title. No exclamation marks, except a closing line such as "Imagine what we can do together!".

## Body

- Short sentences, active voice. Lead with the point.
- Parallel items get parallel grammar: every card title a noun, or every one a verb.
- Numbers with units and, where it matters, a source and a date: "-35% median handling time (pilot, Q1 2027)".
- Name the client and the people. "Your claims handlers", not "the stakeholders".
- No filler: "leverage", "synergies", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "robust" add nothing.

## Fields

- **caption**: two or three words naming the topic.
- **title**: the claim.
- **subtitle**: the qualifier or the "so what", in a few words.
- **intro**: one or two sentences of framing, never a paragraph.
- **card body**: one or two sentences; the template's example shows the length it was designed for.
- **quote**: the person's own words, attributed by role if not by name; highlight the key phrase with `**…**`.

## Header and footer

- Header: `studio` (the practice or studio presenting), `name` (the deck's short name), `page`.
- Footer: `col1` the deck's name, `col2` "Prepared for <Client>", `copyright` "© <year>". Optional `col3`: the date.

## Don't

- Lorem ipsum, "Title", "Caption", "Name Surname" or any template placeholder in a delivered deck.
- Emoji on slides.
- Invented statistics, testimonials or client logos.
